15 Windows Keyboard Shortcuts That Save Time Every Day

November 20, 2026 · Tech Tank

Most people know copy and paste, but Windows has many more shortcuts built right into your keyboard. In this guide you'll learn fifteen that save time every day: clipboard history, pasting without formatting, quick screenshots, snapping windows side by side, reopening a closed tab, voice typing, and the shortcut that rescues a frozen app.

Fifteen Windows shortcuts that save you time every day. No apps to install. Just your keyboard. Most people know copy and paste. But Windows hides a lot more than that. Stay to the end for the rescue shortcut. It saves you when an app freezes.

Why shortcuts matter

Why bother learning shortcuts? Because small actions add up. Reaching for the mouse takes a second or two. Hunting through menus takes even longer. Do that hundreds of times a day. That's real time, and real focus, gone. You don't need to learn all fifteen today. Pick two or three that fit your work. Use them for a week, and they stick. One quick note before we start. These are for Windows 11. Most of them work on Windows 10 too. The Windows key is the one with the logo. Let's start with copy and paste.

1. Clipboard history

Shortcut one: Windows key plus V. Normally, you paste only your last copy. Copy something new, and the old one is gone. Windows key plus V opens your clipboard history. It shows the things you copied recently. Click any one to paste it. The first time, Windows asks you to turn it on. Turn it on, and you're set. It's also in Settings, System, Clipboard. It keeps up to twenty five items. The list clears when you restart your PC. But pinned items stay. So pin things you paste often. Like your email address, or a standard reply. Copied something private? Delete it from the list when you're done.

2. Paste without the formatting

Shortcut two fixes messy pasting. You copy text from a website into an email. And it arrives with strange fonts and colors. Try Control plus Shift plus V instead. It pastes plain text only. So it matches the place you paste it. This works in most web browsers. Like Gmail and Google Docs in your browser. Some desktop apps handle it differently. So test it once in the apps you use most.

3. Screenshots done right

Shortcuts three and four are for screenshots. Windows key plus Shift plus S. Your screen dims, and a small toolbar appears. Drag a box around what you need. It's copied, ready to paste anywhere. Into a chat, an email, or a document. No cropping later. Want the whole screen saved as a file? Press Windows key plus Print Screen. The screen dims for a moment. The image is saved in Pictures, in Screenshots. Great for receipts and confirmation pages. Here's a smart combo. Take a few snips, then press Windows key plus V. Your recent snips are in your clipboard history too.

4. Tame your windows

Next, three shortcuts for a messy screen. Shortcut five: Windows key plus Left arrow. The window snaps to the left half. Windows key plus Right arrow snaps it right. Windows then offers your other open windows. Click one to fill the other half. Now you have two apps side by side. Perfect for copying from one into another. Shortcut six: Windows key plus D. Every window gets out of the way. Your desktop appears. Press it again, and everything comes back. Shortcut seven: Alt plus Tab. Hold Alt, then tap Tab. Each tap moves to the next window. Let go of Alt to open it. Much faster than clicking the taskbar.

5. Browser tab tricks

Now, your web browser. Two shortcuts. Shortcut eight is a favorite. Closed a tab by mistake? Press Control plus Shift plus T. The tab comes right back. Press it again to bring back the one before. It works in Chrome, Edge and Firefox. Shortcut nine: Control plus W. It closes the tab you're on. No need to aim for the tiny X. And if you close the wrong one? You already know the fix.

6. Faster typing

This next part is the most useful for typing. Shortcut ten: Control plus Backspace. It deletes a whole word at once. Not one letter at a time. It works in most apps with a text box. Shortcut eleven: Windows key plus period. That opens the emoji panel. Start typing a word like smile to search. It also has symbols and kaomoji. Handy in apps without an emoji button. Shortcut twelve: Windows key plus H. This opens voice typing. Click in a text box first. Then just talk, and Windows types it. Great for long messages and first drafts. Read it over before you send it. Voice typing can mishear names.

7. Open and lock

Shortcut thirteen: Windows key plus E. File Explorer opens right away. No more digging through the Start menu. Need your Downloads folder? Two seconds. Shortcut fourteen: Windows key plus L. Your PC locks instantly. Your apps stay open behind the lock screen. Use it every time you stand up. At the office, a coffee shop, or at home. Nobody can read your screen while you're away. And nothing you were working on closes.

8. The rescue shortcut

And now, the rescue shortcut. An app freezes. Nothing responds. Press Control plus Shift plus Escape. Task Manager opens directly. Find the app that's not responding. Select it, then click End task. The frozen app closes. The rest of your PC keeps working. One warning here. Unsaved work in that app may be lost. So save often, with Control plus S. And only end apps you recognize.

Quick recap

Quick recap. Here are all fifteen. Windows key plus V: clipboard history. Control plus Shift plus V: plain paste. Windows key plus Shift plus S: snip. Windows key plus Print Screen: full screenshot. Windows key plus arrows: snap windows. Windows key plus D: show desktop. Alt plus Tab: switch windows. Control plus Shift plus T: reopen a tab. Control plus W: close a tab. Control plus Backspace: delete a word. Windows key plus period: emoji. Windows key plus H: voice typing. Windows key plus E: File Explorer. Windows key plus L: lock your PC. Control plus Shift plus Escape: Task Manager. Pick two, and use them all week.
